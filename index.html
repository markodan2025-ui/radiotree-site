It keeps playing

The part that decides whether you keep the app.

A radio that stops is not a radio. This one was tested across five televisions and three versions of Android until it stopped stopping.

  • Background playback with full Android TV media controls, station logo and live song titles on the home screen
  • Automatic reconnection with backoff when a stream drops — it comes back on its own, without you reaching for the remote
  • The station list is cached on the device, so the app still works when the catalogue behind it does not
  • Stations that redirect between http and https now play instead of failing silently, which quietly revived a slice of the catalogue
Editing, on a television

A few phone apps let you tidy your list. None of them live on your TV.

Search any forum and you find the same complaint about the big television radio apps, going back years: you cannot rename anything, you cannot reorder anything, you cannot get rid of what you never listen to. The usual advice to people who ask is to decompile the app and fix it themselves. RadioTree simply lets you do it, with the remote in your hand.

  • Rename any station. Not a folder, not a favourite group — the station itself, to whatever you actually call it
  • Hide what you never use. Individual stations, or entire countries. Every one of them comes back the moment you want it
  • Move a station into another genre when the catalogue files jazz under pop, as it often does
  • Reorder the tabs into the order you listen in, and pin the countries you return to
  • Add your own stream addresses, for the station the catalogue has never heard of
  • Bloated catalogue names — bitrates, web addresses, “24/7 BEST HITS” — are cleaned up on sight, while the real name stays intact for searching

Sound

Honest about what it can and cannot do.

FLAC decoding is built from Google's own media library rather than bolted on. Pure Direct switches every stage of processing off, and greys out the equaliser while it is on, so the signal path is not a matter of belief.

What you will not find here is a claim of bit-perfect output. Android televisions run their audio at a fixed rate and no app can change that. We would rather say so than sell you a number.

What it does not do

A short list, deliberately.

  • No advertising, ever — not banners, not audio spots
  • No analytics, no crash reporting, no tracking of any kind
  • No account, no registration, no email address required
  • No subscription — one payment and the app is yours
  • No data leaves your television. There is no server of ours to send it to.
